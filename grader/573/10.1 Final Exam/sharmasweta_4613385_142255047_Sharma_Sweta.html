Answer - Advantages¶
  • Skewed data is not a problem anymore. Even though mean for affairs is 0.7, max is 57. With binary, whether it's 1 affair or 57, it won't matter.
  • Modelling or visualization will be simplified.
  • If we just want to focus on whether affair is done and not how many times it's done, this binary model is helpful
Answer - Disadvantages¶
  • If we want more clarity on the behavior of someone doing affairs, binary model won't be able to provide much data. Whether someone is doing affairs constantly or if was a one-time thing.
  • Complex visualizations might not be possible. For example, if we want to check whether the number of affairs increased with age or number of children, it won't be possible.

Instagram: Classifying High vs Low Engagement¶

One real-life application of classification is on Instagram, where we might want to predict whether a new post will get high engagement or low engagement. To make this prediction, we could use predictors such as the number of hashtags, the type of post (Reel, image, or carousel), the posting time, and even the length of the caption. Another useful classification example comes from the Indian travel industry. Travel companies might want to classify whether a tourist is likely to revisit a destination, such as Goa, Manali, or Jaipur. The response in this scenario is whether the person will revisit or not. Predictors could include the traveller’s budget, their satisfaction rating from the trip, the type of group they travelled with (solo, family, or friends), and even the weather conditions during their visit. This application involves both inference and prediction: companies want to predict future behaviour, but they are also interested in understanding which factors make people more likely to return to a destination.

Indian Politics: Classifying Voter Support¶

A third example comes from Indian politics, where survey agencies or election teams may classify whether a voter is likely to support a particular political party. The response variable would be the voter’s preferred party. Some reasonable predictors for this task include age, location (urban or rural), education level, past voting history, and the political issues the voter cares about. Here, the goal is mostly prediction because the main objective is to forecast voter preferences before an election. These predictions help political teams plan their campaigns and understand which groups they need to reach out to more effectively.

(c) What are the advantages and disadvantages of a very flexible (versus a less flexible) approach for regression or classification? Under what circumstances might a more flexible approach be preferred to a less flexible approach? When might a less flexible approach be preferred?

Answer¶

A very flexible approach can fit complicated patterns in the data. The main advantage is that it usually gives high prediction accuracy, especially when the relationship between predictors and the response is very nonlinear or hard to capture with simple methods. However, the disadvantage is that these models can be hard to interpret. It’s difficult to explain exactly why the model made a certain prediction.

On the other hand, a less flexible approach is simpler and much easier to interpret. The disadvantage is that they may not capture complex relationships well, so prediction accuracy might be lower if the real-world relationship is nonlinear or complicated.

A more flexible model is preferred when the main goal is prediction, when the underlying pattern is complex, and when we have a large dataset to train on. For example, predicting Instagram engagement or Seattle weather might benefit from flexible models. A less flexible model is preferred when the main goal is interpretation or inference, when we want to understand how variables are related, or when we have limited data. This is useful in situations like understanding which travel factors influence trip cost or analyzing voting behavior.
